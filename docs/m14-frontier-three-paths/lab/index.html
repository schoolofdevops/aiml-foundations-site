Lab — Choose Your Path, Draft Your Next Six Months

Goal: Turn the lesson's three-path comparison into your own decision, backed by a real reason, and a concrete plan for the next six months. This is a self-assessment, not a coding lab — there's no environment to set up and nothing to run.

Time: ~20 minutes Prerequisites: none — this lab is reading and writing, not code. If you haven't cloned the labs repo yet, see setup/environment first, though it isn't required for this particular lab.

Step 1 — Work through the self-assessment worksheet​

From the root of the course repo, open the worksheet:

cd labs/m14
cat path-worksheet.md

The worksheet has three parts, in order:

  1. A short goals questionnaire — six questions about what you've actually run into at work, what you're curious about when something breaks, and how you'd rate your interest in each path. Answer for yourself, not for how it'll sound to anyone else.
  2. A decision guide — a table that lines your questionnaire answers up against MLOps, LLMOps, and Agentic DevOps: what breaks in each world, what you'd own, and which of this course's labs already prepared you for it.
  3. A six-month plan template — the path you're choosing, your honest reason, your current head start, your real gap, your first concrete step this month, and what real progress looks like by month six.

Work through all three parts in the file itself, or copy it to your own working copy first:

cp path-worksheet.md my-path-plan.md

Fill in my-path-plan.md (or the original, if you'd rather edit in place) directly — every blank is meant to be typed into.

Step 2 — Check your answer against the decision guide, not your gut​

Once you've filled in Part 1, go back to Part 2's table before you commit to a path in Part 3. It's easy to pick the path that sounds the most senior or the most in-demand; the table is there specifically to pull you back to which row your own answers actually landed in. If Question 2 and Question 5 in Part 1 point at different rows, reread Question 6 — the incident you'd genuinely want to be paged for is usually the honest tiebreaker.

There's no wrong answer here

This worksheet isn't graded and there's no answer key, because the "right" answer is whichever one is true for you. The point of writing it down instead of just thinking it is the same reason Module 2's glossary asked you to write definitions in your own words: a plan you can't restate in your own sentences isn't a real plan yet.

Common Pitfalls​

Picking the path that sounds most impressive instead of the one you'll actually enjoy

It's tempting to pick "Agentic DevOps" because it sounds like the newest, highest-ceiling option, even if your Part 1 answers all pointed at MLOps or LLMOps. The comparison table in the lesson exists precisely because these are different daily jobs with different kinds of on-call incidents, and the one that matches your actual curiosity will hold your attention longer than the one that matches a job title you saw on a posting.

Treating this as a permanent, unchangeable choice

The lesson is explicit that most engineers doing this work for real end up touching two of the three paths within their first year, because production systems rarely stay inside one row of the table. Pick a deliberate starting point, not a life sentence — Part 3's plan is a six-month plan, not a ten-year one.

Writing vague answers in Part 3 instead of specific ones

"Get better at MLOps" isn't a plan you can check yourself against in six months. "Finish the first two modules of the follow-on MLOps course and set up a drift-monitoring script on a toy model" is. If a blank in Part 3 doesn't have a way to tell whether you actually did it, rewrite it until it does.

Running Step 1's commands from the wrong directory

Both commands assume you're inside labs/m14/. If cat path-worksheet.md or cp path-worksheet.md my-path-plan.md reports No such file or directory, run cd labs/m14 from the repo root first, then retry.

Teardown​

Nothing to tear down here — no processes, no environments, no cloud resources. This lab reads and writes a plain text file. Keep labs/m14/my-path-plan.md (or your edited path-worksheet.md) somewhere you'll actually look at it again in six months — that's the whole point of writing it down.
